How it works

Input: the back drone video, calibrated by BirdsCourt. Four 2D detectors are the only inputs: the court camera, TrackNetV3 for the shuttle, YOLOv8x-pose for the players and YOLOv8x for rackets. Every detection becomes a camera ray in court coordinates, so the model never sees pixels or which way the camera points.

3D: One 6.6-million-parameter transformer reads every player, the shuttle and the rackets across the whole rally, and drives a small physical model of it. Each player is a skeleton with fixed bone lengths, held up only while a foot is on the floor. Each racket is a rigid stick in one hand, the model's choice for the whole rally, turned by the wrist, and it only counts a racket detection that is within reach of that wrist. The model also says who hits when. It was trained on about 4,400 broadcast rallies. During training only, VIMO showed it how each bone runs toward or away from the camera, wherever VIMO agreed with the 2D keypoints; triangulated poses from a few separate drone matches helped too. At run time it needs neither. The shuttle is a chain of gravity-and-drag flights between the model's hits, starting at the hitter's racket and fitted to the shuttle detections; the hitters' court sides come from the model, and a flight that cannot explain its detections gets the hit the model was least sure of. The drone rallies shown here were never trained on.

Ground truth (white) is the shuttle triangulated from both drones; the white floor rings are the players as annotated in the top drone view. The pipeline never sees the top drone. On the video, the cyan dot is the TrackNet detection and the yellow ring is the 3D fit projected back into the image.
