01The idea

Gravity is the only free force

Everything falls. Anything else needs a contact.

One camera cannot tell how far away something is. A rally can. Gravity is known, so the shape of the shuttle's arc over time fixes its distance in metres. Each hit then hands that distance to the hitter's racket head, the racket to the wrist, and the wrist through fixed-length bones to the whole body. The shuttle measures the players, and the players explain the shuttle.

So we don't regress 3D points. One small network reads the whole rally and sets the controls of a physical model: joint angles for a skeleton whose bones cannot stretch, a grip angle for a rigid racket, and the moments when someone hits. Physics fills in everything between those moments.

03Laws of the model

What the model is not allowed to do

Some rules are built into the model's shape, so no output can break them. The rest are terms of one training objective, which the network learns to satisfy across thousands of rallies. Nothing is tuned per rally at test time.

  1. A player's bones keep their length.

    The network outputs joint angles. Forward kinematics turns them into joints, so arms cannot stretch and shins cannot shrink.

    structure

  2. Nobody floats.

    A body is held up only while a foot is on the floor. In the air the pelvis must fall at g, and the floor cannot be passed through.

    objective

  3. A racket is rigid and stays in one hand.

    It is set by an angle in the forearm's own frame, so it moves with the wrist. Which hand holds it is learned once per player for the rally.

    structure

  4. A racket only claims what its hand can reach.

    A racket detection counts for a hand only if it lies within reach of that hand's detected wrist, so an arm is never pulled onto another player's racket.

    objective

  5. Between hits, the shuttle flies freely.

    Gravity and quadratic air drag only, with a terminal speed of 6.4 m/s measured from two-view flights. Consecutive flights share their endpoints.

    structure

  6. A hit happens at the hitter's racket.

    At the moment of a hit, the shuttle must be at the racket head of the player who struck it. This is how the shuttle's depth reaches the body.

    objective

  7. What is seen must lie on its ray.

    Every joint, racket head and shuttle must project onto its detection, with robust losses so a wrong detection cannot pull hard.

    objective
04Data

Trained on broadcast, judged from above

Learns from

  • Broadcast rallies (BFMD, FineBadminton): no 3D labels at all, only the laws above.
  • Hit labels where a dataset has them: who hit and when.
  • Two-drone poses triangulated on separate DroneData matches, plus a small pose prior learned from them.
  • VIMO bone directions during training only, and only where VIMO agrees with the 2D keypoints. It settles whether a forearm points toward or away from the camera.

Judged by

  • 3D error on held-out drone matches. The model sees the back drone; the top drone gives the ground truth.
  • Player position against players annotated in the top view.
  • Hits recovered against labelled hits.
  • Stroke recognition on BFMD. A stroke classifier trained on our 3D output, as a test of how much the representation holds.

At run time the model needs only the four detectors: no VIMO, no SMPL and no second camera.

Run it

python tools/drone_birdscourt.py BD15
python tools/run_tracknet.py BD15 && python tools/run_pose.py BD15 && python tools/run_racket.py BD15

# detections -> rays, then train and evaluate
python tools/build_obs.py drone
TAG=p11 python tools/train_rallynet.py train
